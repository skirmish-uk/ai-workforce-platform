# Customer Onboarding Playbook

## Objective

Turn a signed customer into a functioning, tailored AI employee with a controlled first production workflow.

## Step 1 — Business discovery

Capture:
- company
- industry
- size
- locations
- services/products
- target customers
- business goals
- current software
- major pain points
- high-value repetitive workflows
- existing SOPs
- risks/compliance requirements

## Step 2 — Identify first workflow

Choose ONE high-value workflow first.

Selection criteria:
- frequent
- repetitive
- expensive
- measurable
- well-defined
- enough data/context available
- low enough risk for initial automation

Do not start with "automate everything."

## Step 3 — Define outcome

Record:
- baseline
- target
- metric
- measurement method

Examples:
- response time
- qualified leads
- tasks completed
- processing time
- missed follow-ups
- admin hours
- revenue opportunities

## Step 4 — Knowledge collection

Collect:
- website
- SOPs
- policies
- templates
- FAQs
- product/service information
- terminology
- examples
- relevant historical work

## Step 5 — Integration mapping

List:
- email
- calendar
- CRM
- document storage
- messaging
- project management
- other systems

For each:
- read access
- write access
- sensitive actions
- owner
- authentication method

## Step 6 — Permission design

Start with minimum permissions.

Use:
- read-only first
- draft before send
- approval for sensitive actions

Expand only after successful evaluation.

## Step 7 — Build

Configure:
- agent role
- skills
- knowledge
- workflows
- integrations
- policies
- escalation

## Step 8 — Evaluation

Run:
- normal cases
- edge cases
- ambiguous cases
- prohibited requests
- integration failures
- duplicate requests
- missing data

## Step 9 — Customer acceptance

Customer reviews:
- responsibilities
- permissions
- sample outputs
- approval rules
- escalation process

## Step 10 — Production

Enable production only after acceptance.

## Step 11 — First 30 days

Week 1:
- observe closely

Week 2:
- fix failure patterns

Week 3:
- improve skill/knowledge

Week 4:
- measure outcome

Then propose next workflow.

## Scope control

Customer requests must enter a queue.

Do not promise arbitrary bespoke development as "unlimited."

Marketing may say "managed" and "ongoing improvements", but implementation must have:
- request limits
- prioritisation
- service definitions
- change tracking
